Hi everyone! I’m here today with a big question for you: how many teens would answer yes if asked whether they’re interested in politics? Probably very few.
What comes to mind when I say the word ‘politics’? Probably many of you think of something like ministers in a government or MPs sitting in parliament talking about laws, taxes and other boring things.
But politics is much more than that.
Politics is all about what happens in our neighbourhood, in our country, in the world.
It’s about what citizens should be allowed to do, what they shouldn’t be allowed to do, and what we think is fair and what is not.
The problem is not that young people don’t seem to care and don’t vote, the trouble is that many young people aren’t really aware of how many social issues the word ‘politics’ refers to.
Peace, racism, climate issues – all the things we care about are connected to politics.
It’s not something outside our influence.
For example, in many countries you can start collecting signatures for something you feel strongly about so that all citizens have the opportunity to vote in a referendum.
In my opinion the first step would be teaching more political education at school.
We should all know how the state works, what democratic instruments there are and how to put them into practice.
During political education lessons, current social issues could be discussed.
Then, imaginary political parties could be created, and mock elections could be held to learn how politics really works.
Fortunately, lots of young people already take to the streets to demonstrate against injustice.
Others use social media to sign and share petitions.
So maybe, the truth is that young people are not dropping out of politics but using different forms of participation.
