Speaking in front of others is not easy, but to be clear and interesting, you can prepare a presentation.
In order to organise the contents of your speech well, it is important to think about what you want to say and to consider the specific audience it is for.
The ancient Romans gave fundamental importance to rhetoric, the art of speaking.
If you want a guide on what to say and how to express it well, you can follow Cicero's five phases of a speech.
In the first phase, <i>inventio,</i> you should concentrate on the concepts you want to convey, and think carefully about your audience.
It is important that you adapt the contents of your speech to those who are listening.
Once you have decided on what you want to say, organise the information in an orderly way.
Cicero called this phase <i>dispositio.</i> At this stage you should select the most important points to highlight.
Proceed point by point, but don't use more than five.
This will make it easier for your audience to follow and remember what you are saying.
Your presentation will be more effective if you pay attention to the <i>elocutio,</i> which is the way you express yourself.
You should find a personal style to explain your ideas.
Give lots of examples to keep your listeners' attention and be as clear as you can.
If you learn your speech by heart, it will seem more natural and flow better.
Try timing yourself and practise by giving the presentation to a friend.
On the day of your presentation, when you are in front of your audience, remember the importance of what the ancient Romans called <i>actio:</i> stand up straight and don't turn your back to the audience.
Look your listeners in the eye, and speak clearly and confidently.
Finally, when you are nearing the end of your speech, avoid an abrupt conclusion and perhaps try to find a dramatic finish.
To enhance your presentation, you can use a multimedia device.
You can summarise your ideas in about ten slides, with striking images and effective words.
