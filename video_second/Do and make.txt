<i>Do</i> and <i>make</i> are translated with the same word in Italian but are used in different ways in English.
Generally speaking, in English we use <i>do </i>for work, jobs and repetitive tasks, and we use <i>make </i> for creating or producing something and for specific actions.
So we use <i>do </i>for typically repetitive tasks we do every day, like household chores or schoolwork.
For example we do the ironing and the washing.
We also do our homework, business or a job.
Whereas we make a cake, a cup of tea or lunch.
We can do a test or a course but we make money, a profit or a loss.
<i>Do </i>is used with non-specific activities like do something, but be careful because we do someone a favour and do good.
<i>Make </i>is used with abstract things so we make an arrangement, a decision, a choice, a plan or a request.
But we also make friends, and if we don’t do the housework and look after the house, we make a mess.
Now let’s revise the difference.
We do the ironing, the washing, our homework, business, a job, a test, a course, something, a favour, good and the housework, BUT we make a cake, a cup of tea, lunch, money, a profit, a loss, an arrangement, a decision, a choice, a plan, a request, a mess or friends.
