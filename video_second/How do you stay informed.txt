<i>How do you find out about the news?</i> I rarely read the newspaper.
I prefer to tune into the TV news, especially at dinner time, you know, together with my parents.
I like staying informed by scrolling through my social media feeds.
<i>What kind of news do you go for?</i> Breaking news, definitely.
I love sensational stories.
I usually have a look at current affairs.
<i>What’s the latest piece of news</i> <i>you’ve read or heard?</i> It was a news item covering the war.
It was an interview with a celebrity.
<i>How did you react to it?</i> I felt deeply concerned for the civilians and worried about the spread of war.
I was pleased to hear this celebrity is raising money for cancer research.
<i>Do you sometimes share the news with your friends?</i> Yeah, I sometimes do, especially the weirdest stuff.
Yes, but I fact-check it first.
It might not be true.
<i>How do you spot fake news?</i> I find fake news a bit difficult to recognise.
Stories could be partially true but exaggerated or not checked before publication.
Headlines may be shocking, just trying to get clicks.
I always click away from the story to investigate the site.
I check the source.
<i>How do you rate news on social media?</i> I think news on social media might be less reliable.
Social media is useful because it spreads news quickly and gives different opinions on trending stories.
And the news items are shorter and easier to read.
