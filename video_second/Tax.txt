So, you know in school you’ve got a building, lovely teachers, books, desks, lights, heating.
Have you ever wondered who pays for all this? Well, most schools get their money from the government which also pays for things like the police, doctors, hospitals, the army and so on; and it raises money for all of that from you.
If you buy something like a new phone, 20% – that’s 20 pence in every pound – is tax; and that’s called value added tax, or VAT, which is money the government gets.
Then if you go to work, some of your pay, above a certain amount, will also go to the government; and that’s called income tax.
There are loads of different taxes: there’s a tax on petrol, a tax on your car, there’s a tax called stamp duty when you buy a house.
And once you’re 18, you pay a tax in the local area called council tax which pays for things like street cleaning and waste collection, and looking after vulnerable people too.
Some taxes are there to discourage people from doing things that the government thinks are harmful, so that is why there are taxes on things like fizzy drinks, alcohol and cigarettes.
Altogether, the government expects to raise more than 750 billion pounds in a year.
That’s an average of around 11 grand (£11,000) for every adult and child in Britain.
Now, there are lots of people who pay very little tax, and some people who pay a lot more than that £11,000.
In most years, the government doesn’t raise enough tax to cover everything it wants to spend on.
This is called a ‘deficit’; and the government has to borrow money to cover it.
Some people think that taxes are too high and want to bring them down.
Other people want to raise taxes and spend the money on public services.
But what would you rather have: lower taxes or more money for services? It’s a tricky one.
