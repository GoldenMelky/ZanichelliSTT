<i>Isaac is talking about politics with his mum.</i> You look very thoughtful, Isaac.
What’s on your mind? We had a debate about politics in the debating club this afternoon.
It made me realise I don’t know enough about politics and soon I may have to vote in an election.
Well, you know that the government is made up of ministers.
Yes, I know that there’s a Prime Minister and ministers chosen by him or her to be in charge of important departments like the Home Office, dealing with internal affairs, or the Foreign Office, dealing with foreign issues.
But what do the political parties stand for? Different parties have different views on issues.
At the moment there’s a lot of discussion about immigration, discrimination, unemployment, poverty and inequality.
As well as civil rights and freedom of expression.
Being a politician is a demanding job and even choosing who to vote for can be very tricky.
So the least we can do as responsible citizens is participate by following what is happening, signing petitions for things we believe in and voting for the political party whose views correspond to ours.
You sound like a politician, Mum!
