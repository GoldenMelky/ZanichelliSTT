Growth, Unit 11, Competences for Life, the Future of the Amazon Rainforest.
The Amazon Rainforest is vital for the planet's climate because it regulates and balances both regional and global climates and brings rain to distant countries.
Moreover, the Amazon absorbs large quantities of carbon dioxide, CO2, which are emitted from cars, planes and power stations.
If this forest gets smaller or disappears, the effects of global warming will increase.
In the 1960s, Brazil promoted the colonisation of the Amazon and deforestation started.
It reached its peak in the last two decades, when farmers cleared an area as big as Spain to create space for cattle and soybean production.
It is estimated that in the last 40 years, about 17% of its area has suffered from deforestation.
Scientists are warning that deforestation, forest fires and the rising global temperatures have almost brought this jungle to a point of no return.
The decrease in rainfall and longer dry seasons may change the world's water cycle forever.
If global temperatures rise by 4 degrees Celsius, many parts of the central, eastern and southern Amazon will become infertile savannah.
More than 1,000 major fires burned in the Brazilian Amazon in 2022.
If things continue like this, the Amazon might not exist at all within a few generations, with serious consequences for all life on Earth.
Several environmental organisations are promoting solutions to stop deforestation and man-made fires.
They try to support research, legislation, public policy, environmental education and communication.
In fact, several conservation projects are working on sustainable economic alternatives, which will involve and benefit the local communities.
We all need a future that includes a healthy Amazon rainforest.
