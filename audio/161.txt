As British people know well, after a few wonderful days of sunshine, we have to expect grey skies.
We all know how changeable the weather can be, but we still feel a bit irritated if we have to modify or even cancel our plans at the last minute because of a sudden change in the weather.
But if you're looking at the weather outside and wondering why you suddenly feel sad or under the weather, there may actually be a scientific explanation.
Apparently, wind, rain and unexpectedly cold temperatures may have an effect on our mood, our body and the way we sleep.
According to a recent study, almost 9% of people hate a cloudy sky and feel angrier on rainy days.
This may be because we associate cloudy days with the disappointment of cancelled plans.
On the other hand, researchers noted that sunshine and blue skies may stimulate thoughts of outdoor activities, like swimming or picnics in the countryside.
What's more, several studies have also shown that sunlight improves our mental well-being and mood.
It's also true that we often sleep worse during periods of bad weather, and you might well blame the weather if you wake up and don't feel rested.
It seems that this has something to do with barometric pressure, which is the weight of the air above us.
Low barometric pressure is often linked to stormy weather and less airflow.
If we find it more difficult to breathe, we might also have problems falling asleep.
Another reason we may sleep badly is connected to sunlight.
Our body produces more melatonin with low levels of natural light.
Melatonin is the hormone our brain produces when we're tired.
So, it seems that when the weather is bad, it's harder to sleep, but also harder to stay awake.
