Expansion, Unit 9, Presentation and Practice 2. How will Gen Z change the world of work?
Generation Z, or Gen Z, born between 1997 and 2012, will make up 27% of the workforce in a few years, and they will definitely change the workplace.
Gen Z are in fact the first digital generation. They use technology in all the areas of their lives. They have never experienced a world without the internet, and have probably never used analog cameras, CD players or paper maps.
Gen Z are also the first global generation. They grew up in a world of freely available information, one-click shopping, and nomadic working.
They have experienced three global crises – the 2008 financial crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic, and the war in Ukraine.
However, salary and job security are not their priorities. While their predecessors looked for stable, well-paid jobs, Gen Z won't accept a boring job with a good salary.
They will look for interesting and challenging careers, and will be flexible and enterprising.
According to a recent U.S. survey, about 42% of Gen Z workers put work-life balance, working from home, and flexible leave as their top priorities when looking for a job.
Gen Z consider communication to be one of the most important skills in the workplace.
In fact, they prefer to collaborate with small working groups. When they are in an office, they value quality face time.
This generation will choose jobs where they can develop their skills and expand their experiences.
Some will travel around the world and work remotely.
Research shows that marketing and social media management are some of the highest-rated jobs.
Gen Z will consider diversity and inclusion when choosing an employer, and will be interested in green companies.
Employers will have to change their workplace and training strategies to meet the needs of their young employees.
And this will have lasting consequences.
