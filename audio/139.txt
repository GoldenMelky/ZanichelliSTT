Expansion Active Citizenship Lab 3 Lesson 1 A Story of Hidden Homelessness in Australia
What is homelessness exactly?
We often associate homelessness with those people we see every day sleeping on the streets or asking for money.
But in Australia, for example, only 7% of homeless people sleep rough.
Most of them prefer moving between shelters, living in temporary accommodation, sleeping in cars or couch surfing.
This phenomenon is called hidden homelessness and affects a great number of young people.
Even if it is a common belief that homelessness affects mainly adults or older people, many young people around the world have to deal with this situation every day.
In fact, a recent survey has revealed that one in six Australian teenagers aged 15 to 19 has experienced homelessness at least once.
And this event has had a negative impact on their well-being and future life.
When a group of volunteers met 19-year-old Luke for the first time, he was walking up and down behind a supermarket hoping to get some leftover food.
When they asked him where he lived, he looked really embarrassed.
He didn't want to confess that he didn't have a fixed address.
Without a job or any family support, he couldn't rent a place of his own, so he had to sleep on friends' sofas.
He admitted that he was tired of living out of a suitcase, feeling like a guest asking for favours.
He was pleased to receive the volunteers' help and support.
Thanks to a charity called Mission Beat, Luke has found a decent place to live.
They have also helped him become financially independent.
Now, he works part-time at a local store, so he doesn't worry about housing or meals anymore.
And he can finally look forward to a happier future.
