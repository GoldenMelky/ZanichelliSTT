It's early in the morning and your alarm has just rung.
You'll reach straight for your phone and check what today's weather may be like on your weather app.
It tells you it's going to be a great day, warm and sunny, a perfect day for a picnic.
But what if those predictions are wrong?
You may be stuck in the rain without an umbrella or be too cold in your light clothes because the wind is blowing and it's freezing outside.
If you check other weather apps, you might even get such different forecasts that you might think your weather app is not so reliable after all.
Actually, nowadays, weather forecasts are on average very reliable.
A five-day forecast can accurately predict the weather 90% of the time.
But a 10-day forecast, or longer, is right about half the time.
When an app tells you that there's a 20% chance of rain on a single day, it means that you might even go out without an umbrella.
We meteorologists use super-computers to analyse billions of data and pictures from environmental satellites.
We then create mathematical weather models that weather apps rely on.
These models divide the world into small areas or boxes.
The size of these boxes affects the accuracy.
A large box may not give such precise data over small areas, but can show general trends, like a storm crossing over the country.
Small boxes may be better at forecasting local storms, while other models might prioritise certain kinds of data over others to generate predictions.
For example, wind speed, temperature and humidity.
That's why two models might give different results, even with exactly the same starting observations.
