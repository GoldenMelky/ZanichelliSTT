Here are some strategies that can help you study better and hopefully pass your tests with good marks.
First of all, you must organise your time well.
Use a timetable to schedule your schoolwork and revision for any upcoming test dates.
Remember, you don't have to spend all your time studying and revising.
Be sure that you balance your schedule according to how much you have to do for school and leave time for friends and hobbies too.
Then, you must set realistic study goals.
If you know what you want to achieve for each subject, you'll be happy when you achieve your goals.
It's also important to find a suitable place to study.
You needn't have your own study, but make sure that you have good lighting.
Everything you need to hand and most of all, a tidy desk.
You mustn't have too many interruptions.
As we all know, your smartphone can be a huge distraction.
It's fine to listen to music while you're doing your homework, but you mustn't be tempted to check your messages.
You needn't always study in the same spot.
You can switch your location here and there and study at a local library or in the park, for example.
You must find your learning style.
It might mean repeating key points, if you need to.
But you mustn't learn things by heart.
When you've studied something, summarise the key points.
Probably you needn't go over it again.
If you're a visual learner, you can use mind maps.
Once the main points have become as easy as ABC, move on to another task.
If you have doubts about concepts, look up the information on the internet.
Another good tip is to develop a support network for studying.
This could be a friend or a small group who may make your learning more effective and enjoyable.
You can also catch up on those missed lessons.
If you've failed a test or an exam, a study companion may help you work on your strengths and weaknesses.
