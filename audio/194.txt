Core, Unit 5. Zoom in on women in sport.
The recent Tokyo Olympics saw a great number of women athletes taking part, almost 49%.
This is a hopeful sign that shows that we can finally celebrate women in sport,
and they can benefit from the many values it teaches, like teamwork, determination, and, above all, self-confidence.
In poor areas, sport can even provide safety from violence, as girls learn to speak up and talk to others about their problems.
The British skateboarding team at the Olympics, made up of only two young female skateboarders,
Sky Brown, aged 13, and Bombette Martin, aged 14, is a perfect example of how sport is gradually moving towards inclusion.
Before skateboarding became an official Olympic sport in 2016, it was considered a second-rate activity that mostly teenage boys did in their free time.
Now, however, it attracts people of different genders and social backgrounds, and in parks and along city streets, you see a lot more female skaters.
Is this because of popular athletes like Brown and Martin? Did they set an example with their passion and competitive style?
Lucy Adams, an ex-professional skater who organised the British skateboarding team, likes to think so.
She wants to attract more and more girls to the sport and break down boundaries between genders.
The world needs visible role models of successful women athletes, so girls can follow their example and believe in themselves and their abilities.
