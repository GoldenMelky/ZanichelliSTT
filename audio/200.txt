Expansion, Unit 7, Making Emojis More Representative of Who We Are
Lowry Moore, a young teenager from Nottinghamshire in the UK, became quite famous in 2019 when she convinced Disney film directors to create a heroine wearing glasses that all young bespectacled boys and girls could use as a role model.
The result is Mirabel Madrigal, the loving, generous protagonist of the film Encanto, a Disney heroine with glasses at last.
Now, she is leading another campaign.
She wants to convince tech companies to create an emoji that offers the option of changing glasses, just like the ones that let us change their skin or hair colour.
The idea came from her mum, who, one day, was looking for an emoji wearing glasses that represented her.
The only one she found was the nerd emoji, but it didn't express her personality at all.
Lowry, one of the most confident and unstoppable teenagers around, is sure that emojis representing people wearing glasses in a more positive way can help teenagers feel less anxious about their appearance.
Her idea was an instant success.
Thousands of teens are supporting her and, more importantly, they are talking about their social difficulties.
They tell stories about how people didn't consider them only because of their glasses and about the bad comments they received.
Only the least insecure ones among them ignored the insults and continued to behave as cool as cucumbers.
Lowry and all the teenagers supporting her request are now more hopeful about the future and the possibility of having cuter emojis with glasses that can improve their self-esteem.
