Foundation, Unit 1, A Day in the Life of an Astronaut
The day in a space station starts very early.
The lights go on at 6am,
so my space companions and I know it is time to get up.
We don't need to set an alarm clock.
As on Earth, every day starts with breakfast.
We usually eat toast or cereal with dried milk.
All we do is add some water to our cereal so that we can eat it.
We always have three healthy meals a day
because in space we float all the time
and our bones lose calcium.
One of my favourite dishes is chicken teriyaki.
I love eating that.
After breakfast, we work out for two hours
to make our bones strong and more resistant.
We always run on a treadmill and sometimes do weightlifting.
After a week, you get sick of it,
but it is important to exercise for our health.
At 9am, we are ready to start work on our space station.
There is often something to fix or some experiments to do.
We observe the Earth, take pictures of it,
and we study the effects of zero gravity on the human body.
In the evening, we have some me time.
I usually chat online with my family or friends, watch a film or read a book.
Jake, who is with me on the space station, doesn't like reading,
but really enjoys playing his guitar.
So, sometimes, we all hang out together, listen to him, and simply chill out.
At 11pm, it is time to go to bed.
In a floating sleeping bag, of course.
