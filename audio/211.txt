Growth, Unit 11. The Earth Needs Teens.
Habitat is an association that runs environmental projects for teenagers.
What it does is to organise trips where adolescents learn to observe and study the natural environment to understand how to protect it.
This year's project is called No Nature to Save It and will involve 90 students aged 14 to 17 who will be divided into three groups.
Group A. This group will spend two weeks on the coast of the Atlantic Ocean and will study the environment of that area,
the strength of the wind, the direction it blows from, the formation of waves and how to predict hurricanes.
When the weather is good, students will go on glass-bottomed boats to observe the stunning underwater world made up of fish, crustaceans, coral and beautiful algae.
Group B. This group will stay in a small farmer's village in the countryside, about 200 kilometres from the sea.
There, they will learn everything there is to know about agriculture and the impact it has on land and vegetation.
They will study the characteristics of the local wildlife and of the plants that commonly grow in that area or are cultivated there.
Group C.
The third and last group will spend their two weeks in a mountain lodge.
From there, they will set off for daily excursions to collect data on rain and snowfalls,
on the formation of glaciers and on the strong winds that blow on the mountaintops.
They will almost certainly visit the inside of a glacier where the rain has created incredible caves over the centuries.
Group C. At the end of the two weeks, the three groups will meet again to report on their observations.
Group C. There will be talks, discussions and exchanges of opinions.
Group C. Then the students will go back to their own schools and share what they have learnt with their classmates.
