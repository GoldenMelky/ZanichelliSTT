Expansion Unit 7 Competences for Life Exercises 6 and 7
Speaker 1
My primary school teacher had a great technique to help us feel better.
When one of my classmates was feeling low or upset, he asked them to sit on the so-called empathy chair
and to express their feelings openly surrounded by their class friends.
Then, in turns, we could give some advice or just tell them what a nice person they were and why we loved them.
I experienced it one day and it made me feel happier and more confident.
Speaker 2
The morning is a magical time for me.
What you think and feel during these early moments can have a huge impact on your day.
When I wake up, I always think of something to be grateful for and concentrate on it for a few minutes.
I slowly let this positive thought enter my body and then I get up feeling cheerful and ready for a new adventure.
Speaker 3
Experts say that negative emotions can influence our thoughts and behaviour and can lead to even worse feelings.
Luckily, this is true also for positive thoughts.
They can create better feelings.
Feeling both positive and negative emotions is a natural part of our life.
What is important is to recognise and give a name to these feelings.
Labelling emotions is the most important self-regulating technique to learn in order to reduce stress.
Speaker 4
One day, I read an article about an orphan girl living in New York who was able to turn her sad childhood experience into a positive action.
Tracey Weinstein was born in an orphanage in Guatemala, so she knew from personal experience that poor children can't walk to school if they don't have any shoes.
Together with a non-profit organisation, she collected over 30,000 pairs of shoes for people in need.
Dedicating her life to charity was her way of helping her country of origin.
