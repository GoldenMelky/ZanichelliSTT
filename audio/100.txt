Hello everyone, I'm Kelly Jones and as part of our Talking About Films programme, this
evening I want to dedicate some time to an inspirational American actor, Forrest Whitaker.
I'm sure many of you are familiar with this amazing actor, producer, director and activist.
He has received many awards in his impressive career.
You probably remember Whitaker from dramatic films like Platoon, Good Morning Vietnam and
The Last King of Scotland, where he played the scary dictator Idi Amin.
While he was developing his acting career, Whitaker was also working as a director and became an activist.
In 2011, he became a UNESCO Goodwill Ambassador and the following year, he founded the Whitaker Peace and Development Initiative.
This organisation helps countries affected by conflict and violence to rebuild their communities.
Whitaker's latest achievement is a documentary set in South Sudan called For the Sake of Peace.
While Forrest was making this terrific film, a civil war was happening.
The film looks at different ways young people help their community.
It focuses on two young South Sudanese people who were fighting for the country they call home.
One, called Gatjang, is a football referee in a refugee camp.
He uses the game to bring young people from opposing tribes together.
The second is Nandege, a young mother who becomes a successful mediator between conflicting groups.
In the documentary, the local people were talking freely about their everyday lives as the cameras were running.
The film shows the way individual action can transform difficult situations.
In my opinion, it's an unforgettable film.
