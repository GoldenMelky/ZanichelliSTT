Core, Unit 6, Competences for Life, Peace by Chocolate.
The Canadian screenwriter, director and producer Jonathan Kaiser made the inspirational film Peace by Chocolate in 2021.
It tells the true story of Tarek Hadhad, a young Syrian refugee, played by Ayhem Abu Ammar, and his father Issam, played by Khatim Ali.
They emigrated to Canada with their family in 2015, after fleeing war-torn Syria and the bombing of their chocolate factory in Damascus.
The story follows Tarek as he chooses between following his dream of becoming a doctor and preserving his family's chocolate-making legacy.
The main themes of the film are family loyalty, hard work, persistence and hospitality.
The director shot most scenes in Montreal, with local actors and crew, and some exteriors in the small town of Antigonish, in Nova Scotia, where the Hudhuds now live.
Here they started to make chocolate again in their kitchen.
The Hudhuds soon realised that the cold winters and small town life weren't too difficult to deal with, and that, with support from their community, they could start their business again.
After four years, Peace by Chocolate was producing tens of thousands of chocolates a day.
The family was able to pay back the community's generosity when wildfires devastated a Canadian area in 2016, and many families were leaving their homes and losing everything.
This reminded Tarek of the terrible war in Syria, and he thought about how he could help.
He realised that individual actions can save many lives, and decided to launch a donation campaign.
He founded the Peace on Earth Society, an organisation which donates funds to peace-building projects around the world.
Tarek's goals were to give something back to society, and to support multiculturalism, inclusion and diversity.
