Growth, Unit 12, Fair Chocolate for Better Education.
There is a connection between chocolate and education.
Unfortunately, it isn't only the fact that this food can improve the function of the brain
and have positive effects on studying and attention.
The main reason is that cocoa growers, the first actors in the process of chocolate production,
have little or no access to education.
Some children have to work on the plantations instead of attending classes.
It is a problem that affects students of all ages from primary to secondary education
in cocoa-growing regions such as South America, Asia and especially Africa.
The International Labour Organization, ILO,
has denounced the fact that nearly 30% of children aged 5 to 14 are out of school in Ghana.
This country is one of the largest cocoa producers and here, child labour is a social problem.
In fact, lots of children work on plantations.
They can't attend school because their families are too poor
and they need money to cover their basic necessities.
Other children don't have to work, but have to cover long distances to reach the closest school.
In some cases, they have to walk through the forest daily.
They arrive late for lessons and this has a negative effect on their performance.
International institutions and non-governmental organisations, NGOs, are working to improve the situation.
They have campaigned to make consumers aware and tell them they mustn't buy chocolate that is linked to child labour,
deforestation and other global issues.
Meanwhile, major companies have started dealing with this problem by making sure the cocoa they use is produced ethically.
