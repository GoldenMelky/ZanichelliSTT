Growth, Unit 12, Communication 2, Innovative School Subjects.
Schools worldwide feel they must experiment with new strategies to involve students by introducing either new subjects or more engaging methods to teach the usual school subjects.
Beekeeping lessons in Russia, folk dance lessons in Armenia, surf lessons in Australia or beauty and nail technology in South Africa are just some of these experimental classes from around the world.
Here are some more examples.
The Japanese Ministry of Education has recently decided to introduce nature observation lessons into the school curriculum.
During classes, students have to sit on the grass and look at the beauty of the world around them.
At the same time, they have to focus on the interaction of plants and animals in their natural environment.
It is a different way to teach science and biology, which shows that it doesn't have to be boring.
In some schools in Germany, students attend special classes, happiness lessons, to learn how to reduce stress and relax.
At the end of the year, they don't need to take a test, but they have to demonstrate progress in their ability to live in harmony with themselves and with the world around them.
A few of the students have then to illustrate their happiness project to the rest of the class.
It needn't be too complicated or too personal.
It could be a video clip to show their happiest day or a charitable activity they have organised.
Through these unusual lessons, schools are showing awareness that educational programmes must keep up to date.
For this reason, they have had to diversify their offer to meet the needs of modern teenagers and make their school time more enjoyable.
