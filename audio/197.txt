Core, Unit 6, Cinema for Social Change.
Can films be entertaining and have a deep message at the same time?
The Media Institute for Social Change, MISC, based at the University of Southern California, believes so.
Their idea is that directors, screenwriters and producers have a responsibility to think about important problems.
MISC formed in 2011 with the aim of promoting positive social change through cinema.
Nowadays, it also gives money to support students and does research on the impact of media on society and culture.
Every year, they prepare a list of the best films about social problems.
These lists include several stories the audience love and comment on.
One of them is John Lewis' Good Trouble.
The film is based on the life of John Lewis, a social activist and a member of the House of Representatives of the USA for many years, until his death at the age of 80.
It was the 7th of March 1965, and Lewis was walking with a group of peaceful demonstrators from Selma to Montgomery, Alabama, when the authorities used force to stop them.
They were marching in support of voting rights for African-American people, inspired by the message of Martin Luther King.
This became a key event in the history of the American civil rights movement.
The film explores Lewis' contribution to the positive changes in American society.
According to MISC, in fact, good films can promote discussion, but also create beautiful storytelling.
All social issues can become stories and scripts, from immigration and civil rights to environmental protection and gender equality.
After all, this is the power of cinema.
It can deal with difficult topics and make them art.
