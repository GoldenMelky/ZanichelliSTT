Growth, Unit 10, Travel to Experience the World
For some people, travelling can be life-changing and much more than a leisure activity.
Experiential tourists, as they are now called, don't book package tours.
Their destinations are usually off the beaten track and their holidays are tailor-made.
Their aim is to immerse themselves in the culture and life of the country, city or place they are going to visit.
Immersion tourism, in fact, is another way to describe this trend on the rise among young people.
According to the latest data, 86% of millennials travel for experience and culture.
The most important thing for them is to meet the locals and share real-life moments.
There are plenty of choices out there.
Ready to know about them?
Joe, for example, is travelling to Sweden next summer
and is going on a sightseeing tour of Gothenburg guided by Mikhail,
a young man from the area who works for a website specialised in organising personalised tours with locals.
Joe is sure his guide will tell him all the curious facts about the city's industrial past and contemporary art scene.
The key word of experiential tourism is authenticity.
So popular landmarks can be included.
But there is much more to it.
Isla is flying to Belgium, both to visit the country and get to know its unique culture.
She is going to try something new for her, but she is still in doubt.
She will choose either a workshop on traditional games with a local expert
or a chocolate walk to learn about and taste this typical Belgian product.
Both Singapore and Bangkok are attracting lots of tourists fascinated by Asian culture,
and Leo is one of them.
In Bangkok, he will move around by Tuk Tuk, a three-wheeled taxi which is very common in Thailand,
and shop at a floating market where boats are used as stalls.
In Singapore, local guides will take him to places many tourists don't know about,
hidden gems like ancient temples where he can also practice meditation.
