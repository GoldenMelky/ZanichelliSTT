Expansion. Unit 8. Competences for Life. Tiny Houses.
Have you ever heard about the tiny house movement? It is both an architectural and social movement.
It encourages people to downsize the space they live in and live with less.
Freedom, low cost and sustainability have become the key words of this campaign.
How big or small is a tiny house? It is between 10 and 50 square metres.
You can choose a mini home on wheels or set it on foundations.
Tiny houses generally have a kitchen with a small sink, a dishwasher, a modest living room and a bedroom with a bathroom.
Front doors are often made of glass to let as much natural light as possible into the house.
Tiny houses require less material to build and are more sustainable because they use around 20-30% of the energy of most average UK homes.
Some have solar panels or use wind power.
In the UK there have been exciting initiatives linked to the tiny house movement recently.
The tiny house community in Bristol, a non-profit housing project, has received support from the city's mayor to build up to 15 homes and essential communal spaces,
including a kitchen, a dining hall, an eco-laundrette and a workshop for a zero waste local produce shop.
In Edinburgh, the social bite homeless tiny house village has built a set of tiny houses for the homeless thanks to the support of celebrities such as Leonardo DiCaprio and George Clooney and various fundraising initiatives.
This village provides a low-cost, safe living environment for up to 20 people for around 12 to 18 months.
Over this time, the members of this community contribute to the life of the village and learn new skills.
They also receive support for transition into permanent accommodation afterwards.
