Growth, Unit 12, Competences for Life, Exercises 7 and 8.
Amir
My school is in Philadelphia and became famous as the first school not using textbooks and blackboards.
In fact, all the students have to use computers, and teachers always use interactive whiteboards.
We have a note-taking app for math and even have digital lockers, which we have to open with the flash of an ID card.
When it opened in 2006, students and teachers had to adapt to new techniques.
Now, lots of schools are adapting to digital learning, and we must all live with the challenge of technology.
Nina
I guess I must be really lucky because I attend a global school.
This is a travelling boarding school with no official home base.
Students come from over 28 different nationalities.
Every semester, we have to move to a different country.
I've lived in India, Japan, New Zealand, Costa Rica, Greece, Sweden, Bosnia, and I plan to graduate in Italy.
I've seen places and peoples of every kind.
We focus on the country we are in and have regular field trips, like a visit to a local Japanese potter, or a 16-hour train ride from Stockholm to the Arctic Circle.
Chester
I go to a Steiner school.
My parents liked the Steiner idea that skills not considered essential parts of traditional schools, such as art, music, or gardening, became central to education.
In fact, in our school, we learn a variety of practical arts, like sewing and metalwork.
I also love the emphasis on music, dance, drama, and non-competitive games.
We have to do academic subjects, too, of course, but teachers assess us on the basis of both our effort and our achievements in the various subjects.
We discuss our progress in teacher conferences, and we must learn through our own critical thinking and judgment.
