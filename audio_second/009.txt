Nowadays, romance and dating is very different than it used to be in the past.
For example, where did courting used to happen in the 19th century?
Usually, in the girl's house in front of the girl's family, as a sign of respect.
The couple also used to go out for walks or attend public events,
and this sometimes led to matches made in heaven,
unlike the arranged marriages of the previous century.
And when was the word date first used in this context?
In 1896, a young man complained in a newspaper article
that his girlfriend was filling the dates on her calendar with other boyfriends.
In the early 20th century, attitudes changed,
and young people started to go on dates to get to know each other,
with nobody to supervise them.
They also used to write love letters and gave something like flowers and chocolates as gifts.
Then, in the 1960s, young people started to go out in groups, to the cinema or a concert,
and when they had a crush on someone, they used to ask them out.
When they were head over heels in love, the man used to propose in person.
Has anything changed since then?
Well, almost everything has moved online.
You may meet a person you like somewhere and then look them up online.
Digital platforms are important tools for expressing interest in a potential partner.
Research shows that 50% of teens add contacts on social media accounts to show romantic interest,
and others show attraction by liking, commenting on, or interacting with their crush online,
like adding hearts on somebody's post.
Teens feel more confident about telling people they're falling for them
or saying they have a crush on them through their smartphone.
You even hear stories of how someone split up with their partner on social media
because they cheated on them.
It's true that any type of relationship can be really valuable,
but it's also true that real friendships and romances require more than just virtual contact.
