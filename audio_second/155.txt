Core, Unit 3, Money Smart Teens.
Have any of you ever heard of financial literacy?
If you haven't, it is time you did,
because before you are out of your teens,
you will have some important financial decisions to make.
Being financially literate basically means
knowing how to make smart decisions with money,
which involves keeping track of how much you spend or save,
how to invest small sums of money,
or even how to choose the best options
regarding student loans to pay for your university fees.
According to recent data,
teens are becoming more money smart.
Nowadays, more and more adolescents buy items online
that they pay for by debit card or prepaid card.
Most are also familiar with mobile payments,
and some even have a bank account.
All of these things have made a hugely positive impact
on their financial know-how.
But I wonder if it will be enough
to deal with important money matters
when they become independent adults.
Unfortunately, financial literacy isn't taught in schools,
which I find hard to believe.
How can managing money be less important than biology
or knowing a foreign language?
So, we need to get information on our own.
I was very lucky that way.
My mum was obsessed about preparing me,
a naive teenage girl,
for the world of money.
She used to say that research showed that women
are more at risk of financial abuse than men.
She taught me the basics in a simple,
straightforward way,
which I thought was great.
She told me about the mistakes she made herself as a teenager
and involved me in family money matters.
I soon found myself searching
for the least expensive holiday on the internet,
evaluating the pros and cons of investing
in an electric car rather than a petrol one,
and even deciding which supermarket to shop at.
So, fellow teens,
keep your eyes open when dealing with money
and, for once,
think of your parents as allies,
not enemies.
