Good morning everyone. I'm Joanna Collins, Professor of Digital Education at Trinity College Dublin.
Today, I'd like to discuss the problem of hate speech and give you some tips to help you develop good online behaviour
and fight against hate speech online. First of all, it's important to recognise online hate in its various forms.
It might be a video, a meme or even an emoji which may offend or hurt the person or group it's addressed to.
It's a good idea to discuss the issue of hate speech with friends and family,
to raise awareness and help stop the spread of intolerance in our society.
Highlight the fact that it's not just an online issue,
but a problem that involves and affects the entire community.
Try to treat others as you want to be treated. It's important to learn to respect and empathise with others online,
just as in real life. So, think before you write a post or comment. Remember not to say anything online that you wouldn't say face to face,
and think about the impact of your words on other people. Some hate speech you encounter online often comes from ignorance,
misinformation and stereotypes. So, try to be open and curious towards other people's cultures and beliefs.
This is the first step to accepting differences and learning to respect others.
Don't share or repost hateful content such as videos or messages that attack or may offend a person or a group.
Help platforms detect hateful content by reporting hate speech on social media and other online platforms.
Try to be specific about what is offensive and why. Last but not least, always show solidarity to the victims of hate speech,
both online and in public spaces.
And try to share and spread positive messages online to promote tolerance and inclusivity.
In conclusion, be a responsible and inclusive digital citizen.
