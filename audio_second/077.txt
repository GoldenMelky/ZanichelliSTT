Every time we're online, we're bombarded with pictures, articles, links and videos trying to catch our attention.
Many headlines are sensational and others frightening.
In some cases, it's obvious that the stories are too exaggerated to be real.
But others can be a subtle form of misinformation.
So how can you recognize fake news?
I interviewed several experts in media studies and online psychology.
The first thing they told me was that you should check the source.
It's important to find out where the story came from and see if other websites have published the same news, especially ones you can trust.
If a story is reported on lots of different sites, it's probably true.
The next thing they said was to watch out for fake photos.
If you want to know whether an image is real or photoshopped, look closely to see if it's been changed or use a tool like Google Reverse Image Search.
This shows whether the image has appeared in other contexts which have no connection to the original context.
Another thing they mentioned was that fake news often contains badly written text.
So, watch out for spelling mistakes or poor punctuation.
There are other typical characteristics of fake news, such as the exaggerated use of capital letters or frequent pop-up advertisements.
Also, fake news stories are often written using emotive language to cause strong reactions and persuade you to believe it.
If a story makes you angry, it was probably designed to make you angry.
So, be suspicious.
In conclusion, the message from the experts is clear.
Don't believe everything you read on the internet and don't share it until you have checked the sources and are sure it's true.
