Foundation, Unit 1, Competences for Life, My Personal Rite of Passage
When I left my family in Canada and moved to the north of India, I did it for love.
My husband and I met online while we were living on opposite sides of the world.
We then met in person and started dating, got engaged, and then married just a few months later.
It's not always easy to marry into another culture, and I must confess,
the Indian wedding my parents-in-law organized for us meant much more to me than just a rite of passage.
Luckily, the Indian community welcomed me warmly and completely.
Like most Indian girls, I used to have beautiful long hair when I was younger.
So, when I first arrived here, I felt I had at least something in common with my new neighbors.
Traditionally, many children in India have their hair shaved for religious reasons during the mandan ceremony,
because parents believe they have to purify their baby from negativity in a past life.
But while it's common to see children with shaved heads, most women in Nagpur, the city where I live, have very long hair.
Looking back at my youth, I guess I was so shy at that time that my long hair was like a security blanket for me.
But after moving to India, I realized that I didn't need to hide behind my long hair anymore,
because I was a grown-up woman now.
So, after I got pregnant, I decided to cut my hair short for the first time in my life,
and to send my 40-centimeter-long plait to a charity that makes wigs for children undergoing cancer treatment.
I wanted my gesture to symbolize the transition to adulthood,
and to mark the beginning of my new life as a married woman and a mother.
And if this act could help someone in need,
then I was even happier to contribute with my personal, symbolic rite of passage.
My new haircut.
