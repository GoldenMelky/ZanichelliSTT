Expansion. Active Citizenship Lab 2. Lesson 1. What is hate speech?
What is the difference between freedom of speech and hate speech?
Freedom of speech is a right recognised and protected under international law and in the constitution or human rights documents of many democratic countries.
But any form of expression through which speakers insult or encourage hatred against another person or group of people because of characteristics like race, religion and so on is considered hate speech.
Trying to stop the spread of hate speech does not mean limiting freedom of speech but teaching people to respect other people's rights.
Online and offline hate speech.
Online hate posts are permanent because they remain on the internet forever.
They can go viral and reach a large audience.
They can inspire real-world actions like verbal and physical aggression, property damage and even more tragic events.
Creators of online hate content are often anonymous.
How can hate speech affect teenagers?
Exposure to hate speech can affect teens' well-being and encourage discrimination and hateful attitudes.
Hate speech can also have a negative impact on their school results and social life.
Victims may develop low self-esteem, sleeping disorders and feelings of fear, insecurity and isolation.
They may feel embarrassed and, therefore, try to deal with a problem by themselves.
What are online platforms doing to protect users from online hate?
Most platforms have policies against hate speech.
Violators can have their accounts blocked.
Some platforms use artificial intelligence and have moderators to look for and remove hate speech content.
What do statistics say?
19% of British children and teenagers have encountered hate speech online.
62% of British parents and children are concerned about being exposed to hate speech.
68% of British teens don't know how to report or flag content online.
33% of young people in Europe are worried about being targeted by online hate material.
