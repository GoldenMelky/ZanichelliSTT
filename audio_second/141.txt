Dr. Sylvia Earle is a famous marine biologist, explorer, and activist,
known for her love of and commitment to the world's oceans.
Born on 30th August 1935 in Gibstown, New Jersey,
Dr. Earle's fascination with the ocean began at a young age.
Her family lived on a small farm near Camden.
As a child, she used to spend hours by the pond in her back garden,
filling jars with fish and recording her observations in notebooks.
Her family moved to Dunedin, on the western coast of Florida, when Earle was 13.
Once there, her interest turned to the sea wildlife of the Gulf of Mexico.
Earle finished school at the age of 16, then studied botany and graduated at 19.
In 1966, she received her Ph.D. in botany.
Earle also obtained her certificate as a scuba diver, so she could study ocean plant life better.
From 1964 to 1966, she joined voyages to the Indian Ocean, the Galapagos Islands, the Chilean coast, and the Panama Canal Zone.
In 1979, Dr. Earle made history by descending to a depth of 1,250 feet off the coast of Hawaii,
wearing a specially designed diving suit.
When she got to the sea floor, she came out of the vehicle and explored the ocean for more than two hours at a depth no human had reached before.
She also challenged traditional gender roles in the field of oceanography by leading the first team of women aquanauts.
This opened the way for more women to take up a career in marine science.
Throughout her career, Dr. Earle has made more than 7,000 hours of underwater observations across over 100 expeditions.
She became known as her deepness for her extensive contributions to marine science.
She was also referred to as a living legend by the Library of Congress and, in 1998, as the very first hero for the planet by Time magazine.
In 2009, she founded Mission Blue, an organization dedicated to exploring and protecting the ocean.
Mission Blue focuses on raising awareness about the need to protect hope spots, special places in the ocean that are at risk.
Dr. Earle's story is one of passion and commitment to exploring and preserving ocean biodiversity.
Through her work, she has inspired people around the world to appreciate and conserve the wonders of the sea.
