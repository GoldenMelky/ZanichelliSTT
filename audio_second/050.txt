Core, Unit 4, Competences for Life, Superfoods or Superhype?
The saying is, a healthy mind in a healthy body.
But people are now saying that if you want a healthy body, you should try superfoods.
These are foods that are rich in vitamins, minerals and other nutrients.
However, there is no scientifically based definition for a superfood.
So, what are some of these foods and are they really super?
Berries are often at the top of superfood lists because they are an excellent source of vitamin C,
which should help you reduce the symptoms of coughs, colds and flu.
They also contain antioxidants, which ought to reduce toxins in your body,
have anti-inflammatory benefits as well as improve your memory.
If you also added seeds and nuts, especially almonds, to your diet,
you would gain extra vitamins, minerals and fibres to boost your energy, bone health and immune system.
Fight stress and prevent diseases.
Eggs are an essential source of protein and can help you maintain strong bones, muscles, nails and hair.
Some people say that if you ate eggs every day, you would do better at school because they give you energy.
However, not everyone wants them for breakfast and they contain cholesterol.
If you ate more spinach, you would get more vitamin A, C and K, as well as iron, calcium and potassium.
These nutrients play a vital role in immune function, energy production and general well-being.
It seems that if you eat leafy vegetables, you will reduce common problems like headaches.
And as for fatty fish like salmon, they can help fight stress and prevent heart disease because they are rich in omega-3.
Superfoods are often nutritious, but there is also a lot of hype, which means we tend to focus on just a few specific foods,
while we ought to eat a wide variety of foods in our diet to keep us healthy and physically fit.
