As part of our Cities series, we've invited Joanne Phillips from the European Union
to talk about two very special European City Awards. Welcome Joanne, tell us about these awards.
Good evening to you all. Nowadays, more than 70% of Europeans live in towns and cities.
We think these people need to live in healthy, attractive and sustainable cities with a full
cultural life. This is why the European Union has introduced two annual awards, one for culture
and one for being green. Tell us about the Culture Award. The European Capital of Culture is a year-long
cultural event that celebrates the richness and diversity of cultures in Europe. Every year,
two cities become the capitals of the event. It's all about the role of culture and its contribution to
citizens' well-being and the prosperity of a city. We look at the variety and quality of cultural
activities on offer. We also consider different forms of entertainment and local traditions. The event
brings a lot of positive publicity to a particular city for a year. Although the benefits last much longer. A recent winner in Ireland has been the City of Galway. What about the Green Award? Green Cities Fit for Life is a fantastic European programme. It's been encouraging sustainability in European cities since 2006. Every year, the European European programme has been encouraging sustainability in European cities since 2006.
Every year, the European Green Capital Award goes to a particular city that has made concrete progress in the past years in favour of the environment and the quality of life of its inhabitants. It must be a city which is planning to become more pleasant and sustainable in the near future. I mean, things like building more pedestrian areas or cycle lanes.
I'm sure cities must work very hard to compete for the award. Thank you very much, Joanne.
