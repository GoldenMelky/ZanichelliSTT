Six months ago, I joined a green club in my school called Green Footprints,
in order not to just spend time talking about environmental problems, but to take action.
Something that has always interested me are green urban projects,
and I'll never forget seeing the Park in the Sky that was built in Manchester on an old railway viaduct.
I loved seeing that people could enjoy a park in the heart of the city.
That place inspired me and gave me an idea.
Creating a small, pocket-sized park in an abandoned area just outside our school.
My club approved it, and so we started raising funds.
In just two and a half months, we had raised enough money to start working.
We also managed to contact the National Trust,
one of whose roles is to bring green spaces to people living in urban areas.
They gave us the technical and moral support to continue our work.
Finally, we succeeded in creating our glorious little park.
Residents, school staff and pupils now have a place where they can play,
take a walk or simply sit and relax.
It has increased plant diversity in the area and provided habitats and food for wildlife.
There's even a little colony of rabbits living there now.
The British organisation Create Streets, whose aim is to help create and manage sustainable neighbourhoods,
uses a rule that has now become my motto.
It's the 330-300 rule, which states that everyone should be able to see three trees from wherever they are,
that 30% of any urban area should be covered by trees,
and that everyone should live within 300 metres of a park or green space.
