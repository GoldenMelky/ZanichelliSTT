Homelessness is a global problem that impacts about 150 million people around the world.
Many homeless people are families with children.
But homelessness is only the tip of the iceberg because it is both the cause and the effect of other problems.
According to the European Commission, unemployment and poverty, migration, old age, health problems, relationship breakdowns and accommodation costs are the main causes of this problem.
Homeless teens often don't attend lessons regularly, so they have fewer chances to get qualifications.
They miss the opportunity to learn important life skills and, as a consequence, they have a higher chance of becoming unemployed.
NEET people are especially at risk of becoming homeless in Europe.
This is the reason why it is important to guarantee certain basic rights to all people, especially children and teenagers, in particular, the right to safe and decent accommodation, quality education and training, and decent work.
In 2017, the EU member states signed the European Pillar of Social Rights.
This document contains 20 key principles to guarantee equal opportunities, such as quality education, fair working conditions, social protection and inclusion, for all, especially for people in need.
In 2021, the EU also launched the European Platform on Combatting Homelessness.
All EU member states are actively working to find solutions to reduce the number of children dropping out of school, and to promote youth employment, education and training.
The aim is to train people experiencing homelessness and to help them find a decent and fairly paid job.
There are many effective ways everyone can help achieve UN goal 8 to end homelessness by 2030.
One of these is supporting local communities and charities.
It is up to you to decide how to contribute.
